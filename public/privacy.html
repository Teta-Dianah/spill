Who runs Spill

Spill is run by Dianah Teta, a student at the African Leadership University, as a capstone project. Dianah is responsible for the data Spill keeps.

Questions about your data, or a problem to report: d.teta@alustudent.com

Spill is a prototype. During the pilot it is only used with made-up test data and by adult volunteers (18 or over) who have agreed to take part. It offers peer support from trained volunteers. It is not therapy and not an emergency service.

What Spill keeps

  • Your email and password, for signing in only. The password is handled by Google Firebase; Spill never sees or stores it. Your email is never shown to anyone in the app.
  • A made-up name that Spill picks for you. It is the only name anyone sees.
  • Your age band (16 to 17, or 18 to 25), so that 16 and 17 year olds are only matched with listeners cleared to talk with them.
  • Your answer to "Is this your first time looking for support?", asked once at sign-up.
  • Your mood check-ins and any notes you add to them.
  • Your chats: the messages, the topic you choose, the moods you give before and after a chat, and your answers to the two optional questions after it (was it helpful, would you use Spill again).
  • Safety records: flags raised by the automatic checks, reports and blocks, and what moderators did about them.
  • When you agreed to this notice, and which version.
  • If you apply to be a listener, your answers on the application, and a note that each in-person check was done. The checks themselves (ID, references, police clearance) happen in person, and Spill never keeps the documents.

Spill never asks for your real name, phone number, location or ID documents. The database refuses any extra details on an account.

Spill uses these to run the service, to keep chats safe, and to see whether the pilot helps. For the pilot, the first-time answer, the moods before and after a chat, and whether you would use Spill again are only ever counted as totals, never shown person by person.

Who can see it

  • You can see your own check-ins and chats.
  • The listener in your chat sees your made-up name, what you write and the topic. The moods and answers you give for that chat are saved with it, so they can see those too. Never your email.
  • Moderators see flags and reports, including the text of a message that was not sent because it had contact details. They can read a chat only by opening a case after a flag or report, and every time they do, it is recorded.
  • Moderators and admins can see accounts: the made-up name, age band and first-time answer. Admins also see listener applications. Admins cannot read chats in the app.
  • Pilot totals. When a chat ends, a copy of only its topic, the moods before and after, and your two answers is kept, with no name, ID or message. So is a count of each flag or report, with only its kind. Admins see these as totals, and a share is only shown once at least 5 people are in it.
  • Every message is checked automatically for crisis wording, contact details and abusive language. Crisis wording shows you helplines and alerts a moderator. A message with contact details is not sent.
  • The developer can technically reach the database through Google's Firebase console, to keep Spill running or to look into a problem. Chats are not read this way.

Safety check (test version only). On the test version of Spill, you may be asked whether your messages can also be checked by an AI service (Google Gemini) for crisis or harmful wording. This only happens if you agree. Those messages are sent to Google, whose terms allow people at Google to review them. It is a prototype, and is not used with real users.

Confidentiality and its limits

What you say in a chat stays between you and your listener.

The one exception is a serious risk of harm to you or someone else. Then a moderator may share limited information, such as what was said in the chat, with emergency services. Spill does not know your name or where you are, so if you are in danger, please also call a helpline yourself. "Need help now" lists free ones.

Where it is stored, and for how long

Spill's data is stored by Google Firebase, outside Rwanda: the database is in South Africa, and sign-in details are held by Firebase Authentication on Google's servers. It is sent over an encrypted connection and stored encrypted.

Rwanda's data protection law (Law No. 058/2021) says personal data should be stored in Rwanda unless the storage abroad is authorised. Spill does not have that authorisation yet. That is why, for now, it is only used with test data and adult volunteers who have agreed to this.

  • Your account, email and check-ins are kept until you delete your account.
  • When you delete your account, Spill removes your sign-in, your email, your account, your check-ins and any listener application.
  • Chats, flags and reports stay, because a moderator may need them to look into a report. Once your account is deleted they are linked only to a random code, not to your email or name. A chat and its messages are removed about 90 days after it started, once it is over, the next time either of you opens your dashboard. A chat where a flag or report was raised is kept, in case a moderator needs it.

If Spill's data is ever exposed, Spill will report it to Rwanda's National Cyber Security Authority within 48 hours of finding out, and tell you if it could affect you.

Your rights

Rwanda's data protection law gives you rights over your personal data. You can:

  • ask what Spill keeps about you;
  • ask for something wrong to be corrected;
  • delete your account at any time, from your dashboard;
  • ask for your data to be deleted, or object to how it is used.

To ask, email d.teta@alustudent.com from the email you signed up with.

Spill is for people aged 16 to 25. Nobody under 16 can make an account, because the law needs a parent's or guardian's consent for that.

Terms of use

By making an account, you agree to these:

  • Be 16 to 25, and give your real age band.
  • Keep it on Spill. Don't share or ask for phone numbers, emails, links, social media or a place to meet. Messages with these are not sent.
  • Be kind. No insults, threats, harassment or sexual messages. These are flagged, and a moderator can suspend an account.
  • Keep other people's private details private.
  • Listeners are trained volunteers, not doctors or counsellors.
  • You can end a chat or block someone at any time, with no reason needed. So can they.
  • Spill is not for emergencies. If you are in danger, press "Need help now".
  • Spill is a prototype. It may change, or stop, at any time.
